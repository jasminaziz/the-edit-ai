## The ToolCard — HOLDS

The restructure answered Jasmin's four things: what it is (description),
what it's used for (job chips), how much (price with nonprofit pricing
beside it), and why care should be taken. On the fourth, the finding is
that the caution register she asked for already exists on the card: the
chip labels are the human sentence ("DPIA likely once personal data goes
in" says why care, plainly, in her register), and the trustee note behind
the toggle is the softest line the site owns. The gap she felt is not
wording, it's order: the checks zone leads with the two driest items
(Where your data sits: US / Trains on your content: Yes) and holds the
human sentence back to third.

**One proposal, no copy needed:** swap the zone's internal order so the
DPIA chip leads and the two fact lines follow as its evidence. The
paragraph reads caution-first instead of data-first, at zero vertical cost.

**Watch item:** eleven elements per collapsed card is as lean as the card
gets without losing the moat. Phone density is a /tools grid question, not
a card question, and wants one real look at 390px before F2.

## /tools — NEEDS WORK

Three failures against the test:

1. **The heading is the audit register as a welcome mat.** "Every tool
   judged on data, cost and whether you could defend it to a trustee." is
   the first sentence a nervous visitor reads, and it fails questions 1, 2
   and 4: no job named, no benefit, nothing pressed. Replacement needed,
   comms-first, the checks as reassurance.
2. **The failures are invisible.** Nothing on the page distinguishes
   DeepSeek (published to fail) from HubSpot (Red but recommended). The
   statement presses the failures as the scarcest asset; right now a
   visitor cannot find them without opening every verdict. Needs a visible
   marker on failure rows, which needs one small data decision (a status
   value or column; the A3 sheet already holds the classification) plus
   one approved string. This is the audit's biggest single finding.
3. **The meta description** leads governance and misplaces the flag ("a
   DPIA flag on every verdict"; the flag is on the card). Rewrite.

What holds: the DPIA definition line (right register, right placement),
the sector toggles, the filter empty state (which already says "comes
through the checks", the ruled verb), and the in-grid template card ("The
tools are the easy part" is exactly the register the site wants).

## Legal pages — WRONG

The sharpest contradiction on the site, and assessor-visible. The privacy
policy describes newsletter capture into Supabase: that write path was
removed on 22 August, capture is a Substack subscription on Substack's
infrastructure, and GA4 processing is not mentioned at all. The cookie
policy claims "essential cookies via Supabase" managing "your
subscription session" (false), never mentions analytics cookies, and
points at a consent banner that records a choice nothing reads (known
gates debt, now a stated-policy accuracy problem rather than a parked
technical one). A governance-credentialed site whose own privacy notice
misdescribes its processing fails the third purpose at the first click. I
draft corrected versions for Jasmin's review; this is content she signs,
not copy-pack strings. Terms of service reads fine at a scan; one full
pass when the privacy rewrite happens.
