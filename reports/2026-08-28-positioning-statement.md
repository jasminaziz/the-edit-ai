## What the site is for

The Edit is the easy, low-stress way in to the AI tools worth a comms team's
time: what each one is, what it's used for, what it really costs, and what
to mind when using it. The checks are why the reader can relax. They've been
done so she doesn't have to do them.

## Three purposes, ranked

1. **Serve the reader:** comms people in charities, cultural organisations
   and heritage.
2. **Feed the consultancy.** Three properties, one model: the Substack
   builds trust, The Edit is the evidence, the consultancy converts. On the
   site that's carried by the standing Work with me links in the nav and
   footer and the soft hand-off in the About line. The directory content
   itself never pitches.
3. **Demonstrate the governance practice**, shown rather than asserted: the
   site runs under its own standard. Automation maintains facts with
   sources and never writes judgement, every claim carries a checked date,
   and the same discipline the directory publishes governs its own data
   layer and pipeline. Jasmin built this system, and runs it governed.

The guard on the third: the demonstration lives in the architecture, never
in the register. A site that audits its readers contradicts the lens it
exists to demonstrate.

## Who it's for

One reader, two moments. The same comms person browsing on a Tuesday
wondering what could help, and preparing the answer for a board, a manager
or a funder. The first moment owns the surface: human register, plain
language, no acronym unexplained. The second owns the depth: the checks
zone, the trustee note, the template, in the precise vocabulary the board
and the DPO actually use.

**Added 13 September 2026: who occupies each moment.** The two moments are
often two people, and naming them is what the rest of this section does. It
does not replace the rule above; it says who the rule is for.

### Two lines, one overlap

AI confidence and sector are different measures, so the two audience
descriptions never competed. *AI-curious, little technical background* says
how confident someone is. *Comms teams in charities, cultural organisations
and heritage* says what they do and where. The reader sits where the lines
cross. The 29 August lock settled the sector line. This settles the other.

**Narrow on sector and job.** That is the moat. The checks only mean
something to someone who answers to a board, holds supporter data and hunts
for a charity price.

**Stay wide on confidence**, beginner to practitioner, as long as the way in
starts at the beginner. The one mix ruled out is the general AI-curious
visitor with no sector, who gets a thinner version of what a hundred other
directories already give her. That is what the 45-row ceiling exists to
stop.

### The primary reader

A comms officer or comms manager in a charity, cultural organisation or
heritage body. The comms function is one to three people and often it is
her alone, with design, web and press in the same job description.

**Confidence.** She uses ChatGPT now and then. She has never read a model's
terms. She has heard DPIA in a meeting and did not ask. She is not
frightened of the tools, she is frightened of using one wrong.

**This week.** An appeal email, a run of social posts, a case study to write
up, an annual report to get out. She is not shopping for AI tools. She is
short of time and someone told her these might help.

**What worries her.** Putting supporter data somewhere it should not go.
Being asked a question in a meeting she cannot answer. Committing to a
subscription the finance officer will query.

**Who she answers to.** A head of comms, a director or a chief executive,
and behind them a board.

**The first screen belongs to her**, and to the least confident version of
her. A confident reader scrolls past an easy opening. A nervous one will not
dig through a practitioner's.

### The secondary readers, and what each takes

**The head of comms or director.** Prepares the answer for the board and
holds the budget. This is the person who buys consultancy. She takes the
template, the trustee note and the checks zone.

**The confident practitioner.** Already building, already has opinions. She
takes My Stack, the design kit and the failures. Those surfaces are where
the primary reader goes once she is confident, not a second identity for the
site.

**The board member or data protection officer.** Never visits and should not
have to. She reads the forwarded template cold, with none of the site's
context around it.

**The forwardables are the join.** The officer finds the site and forwards
the template to the person who buys. That is why the second-moment artefacts
get the investment, and it is what makes the mixed audience work rather than
split the site. It also settles which surface serves whom: the homepage and
the directory answer to the officer, the template and the checks answer to
the leader.

### Who it's not for

Named, so the answer is the same every time the question comes back:

- **The general AI-curious visitor with no sector.** Welcome to read.
  Nothing is built for her.
- **Corporate marketing teams, agencies and startups.** The charity price,
  the board sentence and the supporter-data question do not apply to them.
- **Developers and tool builders.** The site judges tools for use, not for
  building on.
- **Anyone who wants comprehensive coverage.** The ceiling is 45 and the
  failures are content, not gaps.

Other AI consultants and competitors read it. That is fine, and it is not a
use to design for.

### Vocabulary

**Her words:** what can I use for the newsletter, is it free for charities,
will it sound like us, where do the photos end up, am I allowed to use this.

**The board's words:** DPIA, lawful basis, data processor, sub-processor,
retention period, supplier due diligence.

The rule is test 5 restated as vocabulary. First-moment surfaces use her
words. Second-moment surfaces use the board's words and translate each one
where it is first met. A board word arriving untranslated in her layer is a
defect, not a register choice.

## What it presses

- **The failures, loudly.** The tools that went through the checks and
  didn't survive them are the scarcest content the site owns and the most
  entertaining. They're the proof the checks are real, and the page people
  screenshot. They render as a feature, never as a concession.
- **The person.** My Stack is the proof that someone real runs the checks:
  first-person workflow voice, the same tools doing actual jobs. The
  failures show the standard works; My Stack shows who holds it. A site
  with the failures but not the person is a standards body. With both,
  it's Jasmin.
- **Prices told straight.** Charity comms teams are price-sensitive in a
  way general audiences aren't, nobody covers it properly, and the
  nonprofit tier is the front door. The site answers "what can we get free
  or discounted" before the reader has to ask.
- **The forwardables.** The template and the trustee note leave the site in
  emails to colleagues and in board packs. Forwarding is the distribution
  model, so the second-moment artefacts get the investment.

## What it promises

Nothing appears until it has been through the checks: the mechanism claim,
sitewide. On the second-moment surfaces the claim states the reader's
stake: this is the reference you can put in front of your board. Facts are
current, sourced and dated. Verdicts are honest and name the catch. No
sponsored listings, no affiliate links. And the reader leaves feeling more
capable than when she arrived.

## What it is not

Not a general AI directory: the row ceiling is 45, and it holds. Not a
governance manual, and not an audit of the reader. And it never claims a
tool "passed": the claim is **been through the checks**, which is what lets
the failures stay honest.

## Surfaces earn their keep

The pass carries `/`, `/tools`, `/my-stack`, `/design-kit`, `/learning`,
`/ai-news`, `/policy-template`, `/submit` and the three legal pages, plus
three redirects. Of these, `/policy-template` is the one route the pivot
added; `/learning` and `/design-kit` are retained rather than chosen;
`/ai-news` launches as acknowledged debt (the feed re-points after launch,
E3); `/my-stack` is pressed above but its framing and the word "stack" on
low-context surfaces are open. Every route passes the same test as every
row, and the audit may return cut or park as a verdict; Jasmin rules on
each.

## The test

Read any page against six questions:

1. Does it help the reader do her job first, and flag what to mind second?
2. Would someone arriving nervous leave more capable, not more audited?
3. Can you hear Jasmin?
4. Does it press an advantage: a failure named, a price told straight,
   something worth forwarding?
5. Does precise vocabulary (DPIA, trustee) stay in the board layer, and get
   translated where it's first met?
6. Does it say been through the checks, never passed?
